Silent day 2026 October

I spent a day with some friends in a private chapel recently. I will write down my experiences and thoughts really shortly.

First of all I am -as it turns out- super lazy, like I had some creative ideations planned, I did non at all, and didn’t even bother to even start. I guess that is normal tough, and i shouldn’t work 699, or even more like i normally do. Less could be more here. Coming back, I didn’t have any productive intentions either. Overvaluing productivity -even morally- I think thus is a common fall of this era, and startup culture. We want to catch a train thats always somewhat ahead in a multidimensional way.

Considering that I have a very poor understanding of whats being worth pursuing in my professional life, research and company building aspirations at the moment; taking a step back seems a natural thing to do at least to clear out some general goals, and search for longevity. A nice question here is how one might build this kind of meditative time into the common weekly habitat without feeling bad about it.

Prayers

I also pursued a unique way of prayer: improvising pseudo-psalms (I plan to come up with a better name for this…). There was one time years ago, when I was feeling really down back in high-school I read some psalms as mean of calming myself, and a prayer structured very similarly as a psalm started pouring out of me. This time I also had the impulse to do so, and being more calm I experimented with a lot of ways of doing it, and managed to reach some great flows with it.

To give you some details I will use a comparison between improvising pseudo-psalms and a common way of practise jazz guitar: playing straight 8ths (16ths) to a static harmony. Both force you to come up with something constantly and relentlessly, for a long period of time. This imposes a unique (I don’t have any other examples in mind) and hard creative constrains.

Firstly we have a somewhat (to me) opaque form: in bebop you build from arpeggios and chromatic connections (although I like some more scale-like modal approaches occasionally) and the accents are clear; psalms in the bible also dictate a style and some common phrases I could always use.

As Adam Neely says repetition legitimises. For a jazz solo to tell a story, repeating melodic motifs are non negotiable. In the case of the prayer, it helped me in keeping a constant flow when I was out of ideas, and more importantly legitimised (for my critical self) imagery absent in classic psalms rooted in my imagination or being weirdly modern.

Another important part is to forgive yourself during the process. I stumbled, stuttered and sometimes just entirely lost track and focus. I tried to not care about this at all and also not to reinforce any self blaming thoughts about it. This turned out to be relatively easy to do, and honestly some beautiful counterpoint is served by silence to the constant flow of expressing yourself here.

To end this with some very general (hackneyed) conclusion. I want to try to practise improvising pseudo-psalms and contemplative prayer as thesis and antithesis, then try uniting them at some point when I feel a spiritual charge for this kind of experimentation.
