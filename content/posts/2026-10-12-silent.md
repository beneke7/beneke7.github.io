# Silent day 2026 October

I spent a day with some friends in a private chapel recently. I will write down my experiences and thoughts really shortly.

First of all I am -as it turns out- super lazy, like I had some creative ideations planned, I did non at all, and didn't even bother to even start. I guess that is normal tough, and i shouldn't work 699, or even more like i normally do. Less could be more here. Coming back, I didn't have any productive intentions either. Overvaluing productivity -even morally- I think thus is a common fall of this era, and startup culture in this day and age. Like we wanna catch a train thats always somewhat ahead in a multidimensional way. 

Considering that I have a very poor understanding of whats being worth pursuing in my professional life, research and company building aspirations. So taking a step back is very much required at least to clear out some general goals, and search for longevity. A nice question here is: how one might build this kind of meditative time into the common weekly habitat without feeling bad about it.

## Prayers

I also pursued a unique way of prayer: improvising pseudo-psalms (I plan to come up with a better name for this...). There was one time years ago, when I was feeling really down back in high-school I read some psalms as mean of calming myself, and a prayer structured very similarly as a psalm started pouring out of me. This time I also had the impulse to do so, and being more calm I experimented with a lot of ways of doing it, and managed to reach some great flows with it.

To give you some details I will use a comparison between *improvising pseudo-psalms* and a common way of practise jazz guitar: playing straight 8ths (16ths) to a static harmony. Both force you to come up with something constantly and relentlessly, for a long period of time. This imposes a unique (I don't have any other examples in mind) and hard creative constrains.

Firstly we have a somewhat (to me) opaque form: in bebop you build from arpeggios and chromatic connections (although I like some more scale-like modal approaches occasionally) and the accents are clear; psalms in the bible also dictate a style and some common phrases you can always use.

To end this with some very general (hackneyed) conclusion. I want to try to practise *improvising pseudo-psalms* and contemplative prayer as thesis and antithesis, then try uniting them at some point when I feel a spiritual charge for this kind of experimentation.
